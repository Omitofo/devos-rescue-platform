# Dropshipping Unit Economics Research

We are evaluating a new ecommerce business built around an animal-rescue-focused brand.

The initial concept is an inventory-free dropshipping model: we will not manufacture, warehouse, or hold inventory ourselves. Third-party manufacturers / fulfillment providers would produce and ship products directly to customers. We would control the brand, designs, storefront, pricing, marketing, content, and customer experience.

Initial product hypotheses:

* T-shirts
* Hoodies / sweaters
* Socks
* Candles
* Keychains / small accessories
* Potentially mugs, blankets, totes, and pet accessories later

The purpose of this research is **not to decide retail prices yet**.

The primary goal is to understand the underlying cost structure and identify realistic supplier options.

## Research Objectives

Investigate current 2026 options for suppliers / manufacturers / fulfillment platforms that support dropshipping and, where possible, white-label or custom-branded products.

For each relevant supplier or supplier category, investigate:

* Base product cost
* Printing / embroidery / customization costs
* Fulfillment fees
* Shipping costs
* Typical shipping destinations / geographic coverage
* Production times
* Shipping times
* Packaging options
* White-label / branded packaging capabilities
* Custom labels / inserts
* Minimum order quantities
* Return policies
* Damaged / lost shipment policies
* Product quality considerations
* Supplier reliability / reputation
* Ecommerce integrations
* API availability
* Shopify / WooCommerce compatibility
* Whether the supplier ships directly to customers
* Whether multiple product categories can be sourced from the same supplier

## Product-Level Research

For each initial product category, estimate realistic cost ranges:

1. T-shirts
2. Hoodies / sweaters
3. Socks
4. Candles
5. Keychains / accessories
6. Optional: mugs
7. Optional: blankets
8. Optional: totes
9. Optional: pet accessories

Focus on the cost paid by the business before retail markup.

Where possible, distinguish:

* product cost
* customization cost
* fulfillment cost
* shipping cost
* payment processing cost
* platform/software cost
* expected return/replacement cost

Do not simply quote advertised "starting at" prices. Identify the assumptions behind the numbers.

## Supplier Comparison

Identify approximately 5–10 serious supplier/platform candidates where sufficient information is available.

Prioritize suppliers that are realistically compatible with a small startup that wants:

* no inventory ownership
* direct-to-customer fulfillment
* custom designs
* international selling
* reasonable margins
* reliable shipping
* scalable catalog
* ecommerce integrations

Separate suppliers by product category when necessary. Do not assume one supplier needs to provide everything.

## Unit Economics Framework

Build example cost models for representative products.

For example:

T-shirt:

Retail price: NOT predetermined

Costs:

* product
* print
* fulfillment
* shipping
* payment processing
* platform allocation
* returns/replacements
* marketing/customer acquisition allowance
* impact contribution

Calculate the resulting contribution margin under multiple scenarios.

Do not optimize for a specific retail price. Instead, show what margins would be required for the business model to be viable.

## Impact / Donation Economics

Because the business intends to support animal rescue organizations, investigate the implications of different impact models:

* percentage of revenue
* percentage of gross profit
* percentage of net profit
* fixed amount per product
* fixed amount per order

Explain the economic advantages and risks of each model.

Do not choose the final model for us.

The objective is to understand what is financially sustainable and what can be communicated transparently to customers.

## Important Questions

Investigate:

* Which product categories appear most attractive for a new inventory-free brand?
* Which categories have problematic shipping economics?
* Which products are easiest to customize?
* Which products have the strongest perceived value relative to supplier cost?
* Which products have high return rates or quality risks?
* Which suppliers have the strongest international fulfillment capabilities?
* Which suppliers are appropriate for testing a small number of products before scaling?
* What hidden costs are commonly overlooked by new dropshipping businesses?
* How much gross margin is typically needed before advertising and impact contributions?
* What cost structure would leave enough room for customer acquisition, discounts, refunds, and donations?

## Output

Produce:

1. Executive summary

3. Supplier comparison table
4. Product-by-product cost ranges
5. Example unit economics
6. Hidden / secondary costs
7. Impact-model economics
8. Key risks

Use current 2026 information wherever possible.

Clearly distinguish:

* verified current pricing

The goal is to understand **costs and feasibility**, not to finalize the business's retail prices or brand strategy.
