Project Intake
1. Project Identity
Working Title: TBD
One-sentence pitch:
A colorful, animal-first rescue and adoption platform where verified rescue organizations can showcase animals looking for homes, while an integrated ecommerce store helps fund animal rescue and welfare initiatives.
Primary owner / decision maker: Project founder / small founding team
Core concept
The project is primarily an animal rescue and adoption platform, not an ecommerce store with a charity section.
The animal is the center of the ecosystem.
The platform should help:
Rescue organizations become more visible.
Animals looking for homes become easier to discover.
Potential adopters browse and learn about animals through a modern, highly visual experience.
Rescue organizations manage their own profiles and animal listings within controlled permissions.
The platform amplify rescue stories and animal content through its own social channels.
An integrated store generate profit that can contribute to animal rescue and welfare projects.
The platform document and communicate the impact generated through the store and other revenue sources.
The long-term concept is an ecosystem where:
animals → rescue organizations → stories/content → audience → adoption/support → commerce → impact → animals
The store is therefore an important mechanism for funding the mission, but it is not the primary identity of the platform.

2. Problem Statement
Animal rescue organizations and shelters often have limited reach, limited resources, and fragmented ways of presenting animals that need homes.
Potential adopters may have difficulty discovering animals outside of the organizations they already know, filtering available animals by useful criteria, understanding each animal's story, or easily sharing an animal with family and friends.
At the same time, rescue organizations need additional visibility and resources. Many have compelling animals and stories but limited capacity for content creation, marketing, and digital distribution.
The project aims to create a centralized platform where verified rescue organizations can present animals in need of adoption in a modern, visual, searchable, and shareable format.
The platform can also become a distribution and amplification channel: rescue organizations contribute authentic animal/rescue content, while the platform can produce and distribute content through external social channels such as Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and similar channels.
A second problem is funding. Rescue organizations need resources for food, medical treatment, shelter, supplies, toys, and other welfare needs. The project therefore includes an ecommerce store whose profits can contribute to these initiatives.
The business is intended to be for-profit while having a substantial mission-driven component centered on animal rescue and welfare.

Security and trust principles
The platform should follow a zero-trust and least-privilege philosophy.
Rescue organizations are trusted participants, but they are not trusted system administrators.
Access should be:
explicitly granted;
scoped to the organization's own resources;
limited according to role/permission;
revocable;
subject to validation and moderation where appropriate.
Public and organization-submitted content should be treated as untrusted input.
The system should consider:
upload validation;
permitted file types;
media size limits;
storage quotas;
media compression;
content moderation;
abuse prevention;
rate limiting;
authorization boundaries;
auditability;
safe deletion/replacement;
protection against malicious uploads.
Exact technical implementation should be determined by the Master Design Plan.
Rescue organization onboarding and content management
The initial platform should use an approval-first model.
Rescue organizations should not receive unrestricted self-service publishing access.
A rescue organization may:
Contact the platform / express interest.
Be reviewed by an administrator.
Be approved or rejected.
Receive an organization account if approved.
Receive limited tools for managing its own organization and animal content.
The platform administrator should retain ultimate control over access and moderation.
The goal is to give approved rescue organizations useful self-service tools without requiring them to rely on the platform team for every ordinary text edit.
Proposed MVP content-management principle
The exact moderation workflow remains open, but the initial direction is:
Lower-risk structured/textual edits:
Rescue organizations should generally be able to manage their own information, such as:
animal name;
description;
age;
breed;
characteristics;
adoption status;
basic location;
rescue organization information;
ordinary animal updates.
Higher-risk / resource-intensive content:
Media and potentially other sensitive changes should be subject to stronger controls.
The initial system may require administrator approval for:
new image uploads;
new video uploads;
replacement of existing media;
unusually large media submissions;
potentially sensitive profile changes;
content flagged by moderation/security rules.
This should not necessarily mean that every small text change requires administrator approval.
The intended direction is:
Easy self-service for ordinary content management, controlled approval for resource-intensive or higher-risk content.
The exact workflow, moderation model, quotas, storage limits, and approval rules should be designed during the Master Design Plan.

Rescue organization content management and moderation
The platform should use a risk-based moderation model rather than requiring administrator approval for every change.
The goal is to balance two competing requirements:
Rescue organizations should have useful self-service tools and should be able to maintain their own animal and organization information without depending on administrators for every ordinary edit.
The platform must maintain strong control over security, storage consumption, inappropriate content, abuse, and publicly visible media.
The initial direction is:
Low-risk textual and structured information should generally be editable by the rescue organization.
Higher-risk or resource-intensive content should have stronger validation and/or administrator moderation.
Rescue organizations should only be able to access and modify their own resources.
Platform administrators retain ultimate moderation and access control.

Abuse and resource protection
Because the project initially operates with limited infrastructure and storage resources, media management is a first-class concern.
The system should consider:

image/video compression;

per-organization storage quotas;

duplicate detection where useful;
moderation;

inappropriate-content detection;
reporting;
audit history;
deletion and archival policies.
The platform should be designed so that an approved rescue organization cannot consume unlimited storage or upload arbitrary amounts of content.
Exact limits should not be invented in the Intake and should instead be established during system design based on the available infrastructure and expected usage.
Moderation philosophy
The intended principle is:
Moderation by risk, not moderation of everything.
The platform should minimize unnecessary administrative work while retaining strong control over content and resources that could create meaningful security, storage, legal, reputational, or abuse risks.
The initial implementation may rely heavily on manual administrator review because the platform is expected to begin with a small number of participating rescue organizations.
As the platform grows, moderation can potentially become more automated through validation, quotas, automated content checks, reporting, and other controls.
